2.1 Doubling a Square In Baudhāyana’s Śulba-Sūtra (c. 800 BCE), Baudhāyana considers the following question: How can one construct a square having double the area of a given square? A first guess might be to simply double the length of each side of the square. Will this new square have double the area of the original square? It’s not hard to see that the new square will have area 2 × 2 = 4 times the area of the original square: So how can one make a square that has double the area? Baudhāyana in his Śulba-Sūtra (Verse 1.9) gave an elegant answer to this question: The diagonal of a square produces a square of double the area of the original square.
