The Big Questions Imagine your school is hosting an annual festival, and you participate in the ‘Young Entrepreneurs Challenge’, where you have to develop a product or service for parents and children visiting your stall. You come up with an idea for a lemonade (nimbu pani) and set up a stall with your classmates. At the end of the day, you prepare and submit a detailed report on the expenses incurred and the profit made to your class teacher. What do you think is required to run the stall? You will have to decide how much to spend on lemons and sugar, where to procure glasses, how much to charge for each glass, and so on. Also, you will have to assess how many people liked the drink and if you made a profit or loss. This simple activity captures the essence of running a business—planning, organising, and learning by doing. This is entrepreneurship, a process through which individuals, or a group of individuals, convert a potential idea into a business venture. Every great business starts with an idea, a need, or a desire to innovate Profit: Money that is earned in trade or business after paying the costs of producing and selling goods and services.
