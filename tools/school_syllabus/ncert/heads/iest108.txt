How do different economic systems address these questions? The Big Questions If you had to choose, would you spend your pocket money on snacks or save it for a new pair of shoes? If your school library had only five copies of a new storybook but 20 students wanted to read it, who should get the book first? Can the students share them or should the school buy more copies? Which type of crop should a farmer grow based on the condition of the soil, rainfall, and market demand? Should an enterprise employ more labour or capital in the production process? Should the government spend more on building highways or hospitals? These are not random questions; rather, these are examples of economic choices that individuals, enterprises, and governments have to make. Some of our preferences are needs, such as essentials like food, water, and shelter, while others are wants, like gadgets, vacations, or luxury items. Human wants are unlimited and keep changing; for instance, people may want to upgrade from a bicycle to a motorbike and then to a car.
