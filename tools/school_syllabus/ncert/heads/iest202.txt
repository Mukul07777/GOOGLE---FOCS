The Big Questions Geography helps us understand how climate, landforms, and water shape life on Earth—and how humans adapt their lives to these conditions. From forests and grasslands to deserts and coastal regions, different environments support different forms of life. At the same time, human activities influence these natural systems in significant ways. This chapter explores the close relationships among physical features, climate, and living organisms, and encourages us to reflect on our responsibility towards preserving the diversity of life on Earth. Imagine our planet as a giant patchwork quilt, where each patch has its own colours, weather, and living beings. These huge patches are called biomes. The word ‘biome’ is made up of the word bio, derived from the Greek word bios, meaning ‘life’, and -ome, a suffix used in biology to denote a group of a specific type of biological constituents. Biomes are large natural regions of the world where certain types of plants and animals live under similar climatic conditions. For example, icy tundras, hot deserts, green forests, and vast grasslands are all different types of biomes. In simple terms, a biome is like a broad environmental region, while an ecosystem is like a neighbourhood within it, where plants, animals, and non-living things interact closely. Each biome is unique, but together they form the living world of our planet, supporting diverse forms of life and helping maintain the balance of nature. Fig. 2.1.
