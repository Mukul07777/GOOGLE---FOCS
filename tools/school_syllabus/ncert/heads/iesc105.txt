Exploring Mixtures and their Separation 73 5.1 How Can We Classify Mixtures? You have learnt that a mixture of sugar and water has a uniform composition throughout. A well-stirred mixture of sugar and water is equally sweet in the first and the last sip. Such a mixture is called a homogeneous mixture (Fig. 5.1) or a solution. Other examples of homogeneous mixtures are vinegar (acetic acid in water), aerated drinks like soda (carbon dioxide in water), etc. A solution always remains homogeneous. On the other hand, a stirred mixture of sand and water is not uniform. The sand particles are easily visible in the water and settle with time.
